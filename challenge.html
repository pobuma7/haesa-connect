The HAESA Challenge System

Discover your direction. Demonstrate your ability. Deploy your talent.

A three-stage journey for university students in any field — History, Archaeology, Economics, or anything else you're building toward. Solve small puzzles, complete short missions, race against your own progress, and walk away with real evidence of what you can do.

The big idea

Not just a competition — a guided pathway

A repeatable journey from curiosity to capability to real-world application. HAESA uses it to understand what students need, recognise demonstrated ability, and connect qualified participants with relevant next steps — without ever promising a scholarship, job or placement as a reward for simply finishing.

Universal

One shared framework

Every student follows the same three stages, but chooses a track connected to their own interests — medicine, law, agriculture, engineering, business, the arts, or any other field.

Practical

Real output, not a quiz score

Every stage ends with something concrete — a goal, a piece of evidence, a tested result — not just points on a leaderboard.

Fair

Transparent from day one

Rules, criteria, deadlines and appeal steps are published before the challenge starts. Accessible alternatives are offered wherever a mission might otherwise need money, special tools or personal connections.

Who can participate, what you earn

Open to any eligible HAESA member

Who can join

  • Any eligible HAESA member, or a student invited through a university or partner
  • Individually, or as a team where team challenges are offered
  • No expensive equipment, paid software or personal connections required — most missions are doable on a phone, with low-bandwidth or offline alternatives where practical

What you earn

  • Progress points and badges as you complete each stage
  • Real feedback from a reviewer, not just a score
  • A record of completed work you can point to
  • Eligibility to apply for relevant advanced opportunities — completion doesn't guarantee a scholarship, job, investment or placement; each opportunity has its own criteria

The three-stage journey

21 days, 7 days per stage

Stage 1 — Discovery

7 days20 points

Mission: find one meaningful goal and choose a practical route toward it.

Missions

  1. The 5-minute self-scan — interests, strengths, challenges, time and device/internet access, and what you hope to achieve.
  2. Spot the opportunity — find one credible resource, opportunity, tool or person that could help. Record the source and deadline.
  3. Solve the priority puzzle — choose the first step most likely to help within your time and resources; explain why in 2–3 sentences.
  4. Set a SMART mini-goal — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound, for the next 14 days.

Scoring (20 pts)

  • Goal clarity (0–5) — clear, realistic, with a deadline
  • Source quality (0–5) — credible, relevant, facts checked
  • Reasoning (0–5) — explains why the first step makes sense
  • Feasibility (0–5) — fits your time, tools and circumstances

Output

  • A personal mission statement and chosen track

Stage 2 — Demonstration

7 days35 points

Mission: create a small, useful piece of evidence that proves progress toward your goal.

Missions

  1. Learn one thing — use one credible resource to learn a skill or concept connected to your goal.
  2. Build the proof — a one-page analysis, spreadsheet, lesson plan, prototype, design, short video, research summary, interview script, budget, or other field-relevant artifact.
  3. Quality check — accuracy, usefulness, clarity, safety, originality. Cite sources; disclose any AI assistance.
  4. The 60-second pitch — the problem, what you made, who it helps, what you learned, what you'd improve.

Scoring (35 pts)

  • Relevance (0–5) — clearly linked to goal and audience
  • Quality & accuracy (0–10) — correct, understandable, responsibly sourced
  • Practical usefulness (0–10) — a usable skill or output, not just a claim
  • Reflection (0–5) — explains decisions, learning, limitations
  • Originality & integrity (0–5) — honestly represented, sources disclosed

Output

  • Portfolio-ready proof of work

Stage 3 — Deployment

7 days45 points

Mission: put your output in front of a real person or context, learn from the response, and improve it.

Missions

  1. Choose a real audience — a peer, lecturer, student club, community group, small business, potential customer or relevant professional.
  2. Run a small test — show the artifact, request feedback, teach the concept, test a prototype, or validate an assumption. Get permission where needed.
  3. Improve it — record feedback and make at least one meaningful improvement, or explain why no change was appropriate.
  4. Report the result — what was tested, feedback received, what changed, what's still uncertain, and the next step.

Scoring (45 pts)

  • Real-world relevance (0–10) — a suitable audience was found
  • Evidence of testing (0–10) — truthful evidence it was tested or shared responsibly
  • Response & learning (0–10) — feedback recorded and interpreted
  • Improvement (0–10) — a meaningful change, or a reasoned case for keeping it
  • Next-step plan (0–5) — specific and realistic

Output

  • A tested output and a next-step plan

Safety rule

Students are never required to spend money, post publicly, contact strangers, disclose private information, perform regulated work, or enter an unsafe setting. A safe simulation or alternative is offered wherever one is needed. Challenges build capability — they never pressure a student into risk.

Transparent by design

Track your progress — and see the cohort's, too

Every participant gets a private dashboard view — your stage, your scores, your feedback, your appeal route. Separately, an opt-in public leaderboard shows stage progress across the cohort, so the whole process stays visible and comparable, not a black box. You choose how much of your own progress is public; opting out never costs you access.

Fairness, always

Published before launch, honoured throughout

Same rubric, every time

Instructions, deadlines, scoring and eligibility are published before a challenge opens — and the same rubric applies to every comparable piece of work.

Feedback and a second chance

Every participant gets feedback and, wherever feasible, a chance to revise before a final score is locked in.

A real appeal route

A score query can be raised within five working days, and a different reviewer handles the appeal where possible.

Consent before public display

Leaderboards are optional. Names, scores or work are only shown publicly with the participant's consent — opting out never costs access.

Join the race

Registration follows the four-part structure of HAESA's own challenge form — who you are, your Discovery puzzle, your Demonstration proof, and your Deployment report. You'll move through the stages at your own pace over the challenge cycle.
